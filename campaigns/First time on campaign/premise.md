Alim — human (or near-human), mid-twenties, independent tech-specialist and salvage runner in the outer rim of the galaxy.  Profile: the rogue-geek. Alim was never a soldier and never a thug — he was the kid who tore apart decommissioned droids in the salvage yards behind his uncle's scrap shop, just to see how they thought. Now grown, he makes his living on the edge of legality: slicing locks and security networks, repairing ship systems nobody else can diagnose, refurbishing discarded tech and selling it cheap to people who genuinely need it. He talks to machines more easily than to people, rambles enthusiastically about circuit design and old starship engineering at exactly the wrong moments, and considers a locked door less a barrier than an insult.  He is not a killer and not a thief by nature — he steals from corporations, crime syndicates, and customs impound lots, never from people who have less than he does. He keeps a battered utility rig covered in self-made modifications and a small astromech-repair droid he rebuilt from three different wrecks and refuses to sell, no matter how desperate things get.  Alignment: Neutral Good. Alim believes in people, not institutions. The Empire-equivalent regime calls him a criminal; the rebel cells call him unreliable; the honest folk of the rim call him when their water recyclers die and they can't afford a licensed technician. He'll break any law to protect someone, and refuse any job that hurts ordinary people — even when refusing costs him the contract, and it often does. His neutrality isn't indifference; it's distrust of every banner that claims to speak for everyone.  Flaws. Overconfident with tech and underconfident with people. Freezes when someone puts him on the spot emotionally. Keeps secrets out of habit even from allies. Believes — dangerously — that every system can be outsmarted, and one day he'll meet one that can't.
